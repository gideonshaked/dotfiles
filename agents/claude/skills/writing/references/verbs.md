# Verb and word choice reference

Read this when a word choice is in dispute. It holds the GOV.UK swaps in full, the Simplified Technical English rules that govern verb form and consistency, and the procedure for settling a challenge.

Do not read it on every draft. The plain English section of the skill covers the common cases. Come here when the author questions a specific word, asks why you picked it, or challenges the register.

## Settling a challenged word choice

Work through these in order and stop at the first one that decides it.

1. Check the GOV.UK swap tables below. If the word appears in the Avoid column, the guide has already settled it. Make the swap and name the entry.
2. Check whether the word carries more than one meaning in context. Simplified Technical English requires one meaning per word, so a word that a reader could take two ways loses to a word that they cannot, even when the ambiguous one reads better.
3. Check whether you used the word as a part of speech it does not normally take, most often a noun pressed into service as a verb. Rewrite so the noun stays a noun.
4. Check whether you rotated synonyms. If the same action appears as check in one place and verify in another, pick one and use it throughout.
5. If none of the above decides it, the choice was a judgement call. Say so plainly, give the reason, and defer to the author. Do not manufacture a rule that does not exist.

Quote the rule that settles it rather than restating your preference. If nothing settles it, admit that rather than inventing an authority.

## GOV.UK words to avoid

Drawn from the [GOV.UK A to Z style guide](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/). Not exhaustive, and the guide itself is the authority, so follow the link when an entry looks wrong.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| advance | improve, or something more specific |
| agenda | plan |
| collaborate | work with |
| combat | solve, fix, or something specific |
| commit, pledge | plan to, or we are going to |
| counter | prevent, or describe the solution |
| deliver | make, create, provide, or something specific |
| deploy | use, or build, create, put in place |
| dialogue | spoke to, or discussion |
| disincentivise | discourage, deter |
| empower | allow, give permission |
| facilitate | name the action, for example run a workshop |
| focus | work on, concentrate on |
| foster | encourage, help |
| impact | have an effect on, influence |
| incentivise | encourage, motivate |
| initiate | start, begin |
| key | important, significant, or cut it |
| land | get, achieve |
| leverage | influence, use |
| liaise | work with, work alongside |
| overarching | encompassing, or cut it |
| progress | work on, develop, make progress |
| promote | recommend, support |
| robust | well thought out, comprehensive |
| slim down | make smaller, reduce the size of |
| streamline | simplify, remove unnecessary administration |
| strengthening | increase funding, concentrate on, add staff |
| tackle | stop, solve, deal with |
| transform | describe the specific changes |
| utilise, utilize | use |

## GOV.UK metaphors to avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| drive, of schemes or people | create, cause, encourage |
| drive out | stop, avoid, prevent |
| going forward, moving forward | from now on, in the future |
| hub, portal, one-stop shop | website, service |
| in order to | usually cut it |
| ring fence, of budgets | separate, or money that will be spent on |

## Where GOV.UK and Orwell agree

Three words appear on both lists, which makes them the safest to cut on sight: promote, utilize, eliminate. Orwell files them under pretentious diction, GOV.UK under words to avoid.

## Simplified Technical English verb rules

These govern the form of a verb rather than the choice between two words.

- Permitted forms are the infinitive, the imperative, the simple present, the simple past, the simple future, and the past participle used only as an adjective.
- No present perfect, past perfect, or other compound and auxiliary constructions. Write "we received the report", not "we have received the report".
- Use "-ing" forms only as a technical noun or as part of one, never as a verb form.
- Use the active voice in every instruction. Passive is allowed only in descriptive text, and only when the actor is genuinely unknown or does not matter to the reader.
- One word, one meaning. Pick one verb for one action and reuse it every time. Never rotate synonyms for the same idea, so do not mix check, verify, and confirm for one action.
- One part of speech per word. Write "apply oil to the valve", not "oil the valve".
- Prefer the verb with a single reading. Write "obey the safety instructions", because "follow" can also mean "come after".

## The official dictionary

This file does not reproduce ASD's dictionary of approved words, which runs to roughly 900 entries plus roughly 1,200 words to avoid with suggested replacements. Read the official document instead when the dispute is about whether a word is approved, or about which sense of a word is the approved one. It is the authority over everything in this file.

Local copy, if present on this machine:

```
~/.claude/reference/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf
```

That copy sits outside the repository on purpose and is not tracked, so it does not travel with these dotfiles. Where the path is empty, download Issue 9 free from [asd-ste100.org](https://www.asd-ste100.org/).

The reason it is not vendored here: the standard's copyright page states that no reproduction or publication of it, in whole or in part, may be made without the written authority of an officer of ASD. It then grants irrevocable free permission to use, reproduce and publish to eight named categories, being ASD member national associations and their member companies, members of AIA and AIAC, other ICCAIA members, customers of companies in those categories, the Ministries of Defense of ASD, AIA and AIAC member countries, Airlines for America, airworthiness authorities, and universities and research institutes for educational purposes. An organization outside all eight needs written permission before copying the dictionary into a repository, which can be requested from ASD at info@asd-europe.org.
